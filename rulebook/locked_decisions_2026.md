## 1. ★ THE NO-ROTATION BASELINE — the governing constraint

With alfalfa out, nothing in the cropping plan periodically resets the soil profile.

**What alfalfa was actually doing:** the benefit was **NOT nitrogen** — see §3, where V15's own
spring profile settles it. The two mechanisms that fit the data are **deep root channeling** and
**three to four years of large water volumes moving down through the profile with no annual
compaction pass**. V7 proves it: post-alfalfa, good variety, adequate water at silk, and it still
collapsed — because sludge put the salt back before the corn went in. **The bonus disappeared the
moment salt was reintroduced. The mechanism is physical and salt-related.**

**The uncomfortable implication:** the high water use that made alfalfa uneconomic is a large
part of what made the following corn crop good.

**The substitute: buy the leaching directly.** Rough shape — verify against operator's own records:

| | Water | Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| 3 yrs alfalfa | ~13–14 AF/ac | soft |
| 3 yrs corn + annual ~6" winter leach | ~9–10 AF/ac | 3 corn crops |

**★ ACCOUNTING ASYMMETRY — VERIFY BEFORE RELYING ON IT.** Greater Kaweah bills on *measured ET*,
not applied water. Alfalfa's extra water is transpiration — 100% billable consumptive use. Winter
leaching water applied Dec–Jan, no crop, low reference ET, largely percolates below the root zone
and should not register as ET the same way. If this holds, leaching achieves the salt outcome at
a fraction of alfalfa's billed cost. **Confirm directly with Greater Kaweah (Eric Osterling's
office) before building a program on it. Do not assume.**

### Consequences — three permanent changes

**(a) Gypsum reclassifies.** It stops being a standalone annual line item and becomes the thing
that makes a leach work. **Gypsum without water moving through it does very little. Schedule
gypsum and leach together on lagoon ground, or don't bother.**

**(b) Winter leaching becomes a scheduled program, not a rescue.** Already in practice on A1.
Extend it as a rotating obligation across the six lagoon fields.

**(c) Sampling must run ahead of the problem.** With no rotation resetting anything, what matters
is the **EC and SAR trend line year over year on the lagoon fields.** V7 reached EC 1.55 without
anyone watching it climb.

**Partial substitute for alfalfa's biopores:** deep ripping on the light, compacted fields —
V7 (CEC 13.3) and A4 (CEC 9.5). Not equivalent to four years of taproot, but it opens the profile
so a leach drains instead of perching. **Trial one field before committing the ranch.**

**Already in the routine and worth protecting:** the **February fresh-water pre-irrigation**
(V15 ran 2/26/25 and 2/9/26). Held as a real leaching set rather than just wetting up, this is
the closest thing in the current program to what alfalfa was doing.

## 3. ★ POST-ALFALFA — revised rule

The old rulebook figure (~2.3–2.7 T median) **understates the bonus.** V15:

| | V15 | Ranch avg | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 (yr 1 post-alfalfa) | 42.02 | 33.40 | **+8.6** |
| 2026 (yr 2 post-alfalfa) | 38.90 | 34.01 | **+4.9** |

Call it 10–13 T/ac across two years. **Partly confounded** — V15 is also the heaviest ground on
the ranch at CEC 20.9 — but that compounds rather than undercuts the rule: alfalfa's root channels
improve infiltration and effective root depth, and heavy ground is where that pays most.
**Alfalfa returning onto high-CEC ground is the best combination available.** Discount by half if
being conservative; still large.

### ★ NITROGEN — the surplus does not exist. Do not cut N.

**V15 spring N profile, 04-17S115463, sampled 04-17-25 — eight days after planting, straight out
of alfalfa:**

| Depth | NO3-N | lb N/ac |
|---|---|---|
| 0–12" | 24.4 ppm | 97.6 |
| 12–30" | 10.6 ppm | 42.4 |
| **Total 0–30"** | | **140 lb** |

On a comparable 0–18" basis (the ×6 conversion used on the July reports) that is roughly
**119 lb** — against **161 lb** measured in the same field **after** the 38.9-ton 2026 crop.

> **The field had MORE nitrate after a 38.9-ton crop than it had coming out of alfalfa.**

*Caveat: April vs July sampling, cool soil vs a full season of mineralization — not a perfectly
clean comparison. The direction is not ambiguous.*

> **RULE: Post-alfalfa fields arrive with a K hole and NO nitrogen surplus.**
> **Do NOT cut nitrogen because a field is coming out of alfalfa.** Spring-profile it and budget
> to the target crop like any other field. **Budget K up, not down.**
> **This applies directly to V4 in 2028.**

### ★ Size nitrogen to the target crop, not the lab's default

Valley Tech builds sidedress against **266 lb total N for a 32-ton crop.** On your best ground
that under-targets, and V15's two years show what it costs:

| | 2025 (42.02 T) | 2026 (38.90 T) |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-irrigation | 2/26/25 | 2/9/26 |
| Plant | 4/9 | 3/24 |
| Sidedress | 5/5 — **200 units** | 4/21 — **150 units** |
| Water-runs | 1 × ~40–50 u | 2 × 17.5 u (5 gal/ac UN-32) |
| **Total N** | **~240–250 units** | **~185 units** |
| Irrigations | 6 | 6 |
| Plant → 1st water | 31 days | 35 days |
| Last water → cut | 19 days | 21 days |

The water program was near-identical both years. **Nitrogen was not — 2026 ran 55–65 units lighter.**
The budget math tracks: 42 T needs ~350 lb N. 2025 = 140 residual + ~245 applied = **~385 lb**
(comfortable for 42 T). 2026 ≈ **~325 lb** (supports ~37 T). The crop made 38.90.

Valley Tech recommended 161 lb sidedress in 2025; the operator went ~80 units over and got 42 tons.

**Honest caveat: confounded.** 2026 was year 2 out of alfalfa and decay alone predicts a drop, and
the 13-year data says yield response to sidedress rate is modest. One field-year cannot separate
them. But N is the only input that changed materially, and the arithmetic fits at both ends.

**2027 action on V15: run the 2025 program — ~200 units at sidedress plus a real water-run, not
150 plus two 17.5-unit passes.** Spring-profile first. ~65 extra units of UN-32 ≈ $8/ac at
$395/ton against a ton or two of silage.

### ★ The K bill

Alfalfa removes ~600 lb K2O/ac/yr at 10 T. Several years of that mines the exchange. **V15's
131 ppm K (1.6 %CEC on a CEC of 20.9) is the bill for the alfalfa bonus — not a manure-program
failure.**

### V15 specifically — stop chasing %CEC

Moving V15 from 1.6% to the 4% floor on CEC 20.9 needs roughly **196 ppm more K** ≈ **~1,400 lb
K2O/ac** on an 0–18" basis with zero crop removal. **Not achievable and not the goal.**

**Switch to annual replacement:** budget against removal (~325–350 lb K2O for a 38–40 T crop) and
accept that the soil test stays low. On heavy ground, a low K test under a 38-ton crop is a field
feeding the crop, not starving it. 20 T/ac manure delivers ~440 lb K2O on generic values, so
full-rate manure covers replacement; purchased potash only if the pass comes up short.

**2027:** V15 enters year 3. Expect continued decay. **Target 36–38 T, not 40.** Keep it high in
the manure queue, best variety, earliest good planting slot, cover the K replacement, and run the
2025 N budget.
