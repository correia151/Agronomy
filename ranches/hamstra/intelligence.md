## Yield × soil comparison — 2026 early single-crop 1718 block

All four planted within a week (3/16–3/23), same variety, chopped 7/15–7/21, yields at 68% moisture. **Block average 38.06 T — first block to approach the 40 T target; field 9 is the first 40+ field.** Early single-crop 1718 lever confirmed again.

| Field | Yield T/ac | Planted | Manure | Post-harvest soil signature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | **40.66** | 3/16 | 20 T dry + manure-water passes | ~225 lb residual N; P/K/Zn all high; **Na down to 18.5 %SPe from 30.2 in Dec-25** |
| 29 | 39.33 | 3/17 | manure water (5/7, 6/1) | ~392 lb residual N; EC 2.88 (highest on ranch); K 1,010; calcareous |
| 24&25 | 37.18 | 3/23 | none listed | Split block: 24 side ~239 lb N, cleanest Na (14.3%); **25 side ran dry — ~39 lb N, S LOW, B LOW, EC 0.82** |
| 36 | 35.06 | 3/18 | manure water (6/1) | Split field: North ~301 lb N vs South ~119 lb; scalding noted in-season; tillage trial (N half no roller) |

### What the comparison says

1. **Field 9's sodium dropped 30.2 → 18.5 %SPe in seven months.** Dec-25 it was the worst Na on the ranch; after a January pre-irrigation, ~9 water passes, and 20 T manure it's mid-pack and made 40.66 T. **Direct on-farm evidence that leaching (not gypsum) is the reclamation lever — exactly the rulebook's position.** Prior rev. of this log wrongly attributed the drop to composite dilution; it's real. Keep the 2 T gypsum call under review rather than automatic.
2. **Field 25 finished empty and it likely cost tons.** 24 and 25 were farmed as one block (37.18 T), but 25's half ended at ~39 lb N with sulfate low and EC 0.82 — the crop mined the tank. The ~3.5 T gap to field 9 plausibly includes late-season N/S runout on the 25 half. Next corn on 25: **keep the 75-unit floor and deliver S as AMS/ATS at sidedress** — cleanest possible test of the sulfur finding. Plus VT's 1.5 T gypsum + 2 lb B.
3. **Field 29 made 39.3 T at EC 2.88.** High salts did not cap yield on an early crop with frequent water — salinity tolerance is higher when the profile stays wet, consistent with the terminal-dry-down finding (avoid long hot intervals, not the salt number per se).
4. **Residual N did not buy yield.** 29 finished with ~392 lb left and still trailed 9; 25 finished at ~39 lb on the block that yielded 37. Reinforces the modest-N-response finding — beyond sufficiency, N stays in the ground (or leaves). 29's leftover N now lands in a new alfalfa stand, which is the best possible landing: zero purchased N, but expect first-year weed pressure and slow nodulation; hold lagoon water off through establishment.
5. **Field 36 is the block's question mark (35.06 T, lowest).** The North/South sample split (301 vs 119 lb N; Zn 2.5 vs 4.7) is a 2.5× fertility difference inside one field — likely uneven manure-water distribution, and it overlaps the 2025 tillage trial (north half no roller) and the in-season scalding note. **Before the alfalfa goes in: record which half was which in the trial, and treat the halves separately** — the North half needs nothing but B; the South half is the one that would justify any starter fertility. CAN-17 was applied to 36 in-season; that plus manure water explains the North's load if the split application favored it.

### Fall alfalfa program (11, 29, 36)

- **29:** No N-P-K (392 lb residual, P 130, K 1,010). 0.5 T gypsum + 0.3 T elemental S (works the free lime — only calcareous ground in the set) + 1 lb B. Luxury-high K — watch hay K on this ground for dry-cow markets.
- **36:** No N-P-K either half. North: 1 lb B; Zn 2.5 is at the row-crop floor — 5 lb Zn is cheap insurance for the eventual corn return if a spreader is crossing anyway. South: 0.5 T gypsum.
- **11: sample missing — get it before ground prep.** 11 is one of the lagoon-scald diagnostic fields (11/14/17) carrying 300+ lb residual with valve-end sodium loading. Going to alfalfa makes the **fresh-water leaching set on the valve-end checks before planting** the priority action — a salt-scalded seedbed will thin a new alfalfa stand permanently, and field 9 just proved leaching works here.
