## 3. Gypsum / Amendment Program (PROVEN from labs)

The lab calls gypsum on **every field sampled** — 0.5 to 2.0 T/ac (10T spot rate on the 12SW reclamation area in 2024). This is the central structural lever (no rotational sodium reset exists here). On calcareous high-pH fields (1S, 4N, 4S, 6S, 7N, 7S, 19E, 19W) the lab adds **elemental sulfur (0.2–1.0 T/ac)**; on the acidic fields (7S, 16, 14) it calls **lime (1–1.5 T/ac)** instead — do not sulfur the acidic fields.

Gypsum chemistry: 1 T of 100% gypsum supplies ~480 lb Ca + 340 lb SO₄-S. Maintain soluble Ca > 2× (Mg + Na). EC > 0.6 dS/m sustains water penetration; > 4.0 dS/m is excessive (6S at 3.49 and 14 at 5.76 are the watch fields).

2026 corn plan applied **Gyp 1T** standard; notes call **7 & 9 for 2T**, consistent with their chemistry.

**Fresh-water pre-irrigation is now a proven companion lever to gypsum** (see §5): it leaches soluble Na out of the seed zone where exchangeable chemistry is sound. Standing rule from the corn sheets: **do not pre-irrigate with Dixie Creek manure water** — manure-water pre-irrigation gives false planting moisture and adds the very salt the pre-irrigation should be removing. Field 7 is the exception case where leaching alone is insufficient: its Ca%CEC (59–60) means the exchange complex itself is degraded — gypsum first, then leach.

Top three Ca%CEC fields (18, 20N, 12) = top three yields. Lowest Ca%CEC field (14) landed mid-pack **despite** the cleanest soluble Na and the biggest fertility bank on the report (248 ppm NO₃, 4.3% OM). Field 17, mid Ca and mid Na with big fertility, finished last — its spring stand trouble ("germinated but stopped in heavy manure") is the cultural confounder there.

**R-block (chemistry 2025 → yield 2026):** R1–R4 at 82–85% Ca%CEC yielded 40.3 despite carrying the worst soluble Na in the R-set (R2 at 53%); R14 with the cleanest solution chemistry yielded 34.6 (confounded partly by 2089).

**Operating interpretation:** soluble Na (Na%SPe) is transient, leachable salt — a management cost (gypsum + fresh-water leaching), not a yield ceiling. Exchangeable base-saturation Ca (Ca%CEC) is the durable soil asset and the better yield predictor. This is the same Na%SPe-vs-Na%CEC distinction proven at Hamstra (field 9: 30.2 → 18.5 %SPe in seven months of leaching), now yield-validated at TeVelde. **Rank fields for variety/timing confidence by Ca%CEC, not by soluble Na%.**

### Case study — Field 18: 45.1 T/ac through the sodium (PROVEN sequence)

1. **07-17-25 (pre-sludge sample):** highest soluble Na on the ranch (44–50% SPe) AND the leanest fertility — K 97–173 ppm, P 8–20 ppm, Zn low. Lab prescribed 50–150 P₂O₅, 150–200 K₂O, Zn, 1.5–2T gyp. Underneath: **87.9–89.9% Ca%CEC — the best exchangeable chemistry on the operation.**
2. **Sludge applied** after sampling — covered the P-K-Zn deficit (with more salt riding along; the pre-plant sample does NOT reflect post-sludge fertility or salinity).
3. **Fresh-water pre-irrigation** — leached the soluble load out of the seed zone before the crop lived in it (vs. the manure-water pre-irrigation prohibition).
4. **Establishment penalty anyway:** "18 came up spotty following sludge field."
5. **Result: 45.1 T/ac @32% DM — operation record — with a spotty stand.**

Three lessons in one field: (a) soluble Na is leachable and fresh pre-irrigation is the tool; (b) base-sat Ca sets the ceiling; (c) sludge can carry the fertility program where the exchange chemistry is sound. Also flags 18986 as a variety to trial-expand.

**Application to 2027 corn (the sampled wheat ground):** the Na>Ca fields — 1S, 4N, 4S, 9N — have Ca%CEC 66–77; their soluble numbers are not a death sentence *if they get gypsum + fresh-water pre-irrigation*. **Field 7 is different:** Ca%CEC 59–63 means the exchange complex itself is calcium-poor — leaching alone won't fix it. 7 is the gypsum-first field (2T standing) and gets the lowest yield-confidence rating until its Ca%CEC moves.

### Case study — Field 12: reclamation arc (PROVEN)

May 2024 paired diagnostic (05-02S83635) found the SW area at pH 9.4 subsoil, EC 23 dS/m, toxic chloride, Na 72 meq/L — lab called 10T gyp + 2T sulfur + leaching. Two seasons later, field 12 posted **41.3 T/ac @32% DM — #2 on the ranch.** Alongside Hamstra field 9's Na%SPe drop (30.2 → 18.5), this is the second on-farm proof that aggressive amendment + leaching pays off within the rotation cycle. It also validates the paired-sampling protocol (bad vs. adjacent good, not book values) as the standard diagnostic design.

### Nitrogen side-note (consistent with the Hamstra finding)

2026 zero-sidedress fields (5, 8, 17, R5) yielded 34.2–37.2; 150-unit fields (10, 20N) yielded 35.6–38.1. Modest spread — sidedress rate remains a low-sensitivity lever on high-residual dairy ground. Keep the 75-unit floor policy per rulebook, waiving on documented high-residual fields.
