Findings Hamstra Corn — What Drives Yield (and How to Raise It) — yields normalized to 32% DM, ,All yields in this workbook are normalized to a common 32% dry-matter basis (68% moisture). Earlier seasons recorded at 70%/69% moisture were scaled down accordingly so cross-year and cross-variety comparisons are apples-to-apples. ,The one factor that dominates: planting date ,"Across 14 seasons and 356 fields, planting date explains yield far more than any input. Detrended correlation of yield vs. planting day = -0.51 (very strong; later planting = lower yield). Nothing else comes close." ,• Single-crop fields (planted before May 1) average +4.4 t/ac ABOVE their year's mean. Double-crop fields (planted after wheat) average about even to slightly below. ,• Even among double-crop fields: planted by Jun 10 ≈ year average; Jun 11-20 = -0.8 t/ac; after Jun 20 = -2.5 t/ac. Every week of delay past early June costs roughly 1-1.5 t/ac. ,"ACTION: Maximize single-crop corn acres where rotation allows. For double-crop, push wheat harvest and corn planting as early as possible — aim to finish planting by \~June 10 and avoid planting past June 20." ,"Secondary levers (real but much smaller, seen within double-crop fields)" ,• Fertilizer N: fields getting \<=100 units averaged -1.4 t/ac vs. year mean; 101-150 = -0.5; \>150 ≈ even. Detrended r = +0.18. Under-fertilizing clearly costs yield; \>150 units performed best. ,• Irrigation count: \<=5 irrigations averaged -2.1 t/ac (clearly too few). 6 is standard; 7+ added a small bump (+0.2). Don't drop below 6 irrigations. ,"• UN-32 through irrigation: fields with UN-32 ran slightly ahead (about +0.4 t/ac vs. none) — small and not statistically strong, but directionally positive." ,ACTION: Keep N up — don't let any field fall below \~100 units; the data favors 150+. Hold at least 6 irrigations per field.
