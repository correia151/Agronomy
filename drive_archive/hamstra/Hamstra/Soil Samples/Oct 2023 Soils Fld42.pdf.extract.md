Alkaline pH values just within & just above ideal ranges. Strongly calcareous, significant to high free lime was detected. Acidify as desired to react with free lime & liberate Ca. Free Lime (CaCO3) is a natural source of insoluble Ca. The soil pH values and the presence of free lime will usually be greater in the sub-surface soil depths. Acidic reactions from Sulfur and/or acidic fertilizer materials will reduce pH and dissolve free lime which will liberate & sustain Ca solubility & also enhance nutrient availability. Common soil sulfur will react over an extended period of 2 to 5 years. Tiger 90 sulfur material will provide a more immediate reaction & benefits. Reduce rates if using Tiger 90. It requires 3 tons of Sulfur to neutralize 1% Calcium Carbonate (CaCO3 "Free Lime"). Three tons of Sulfuric Acid will provide the same reaction as 1 ton of Sulfur. Review past and current acidification programs and assess reactivity before applying additional Sulfur materials. Maintain soluble salts (EC) above 0.6 dS/m to sustain water penetration. Total salt concentrations above 4.0 dS/m are considered to be excessive. Moderate salt concentrations (EC) are present. Maintain soluble Calcium (Ca) greater than TWICE soluble Magnesium (Mg) & soluble Sodium (Na). Maintaining soluble Ca will benefit water penetration, drainage & also enhance nutrient interactions and nutrient availability. 100% Gypsum contains 24% Ca & 17% Sulfur as Sulfate, 1 ton will supply 480 lbs Ca & 340 lbs SO4-S. Gypsum has an alkaline pH of 7.8 and can contain 1 to 2% lime and trace Boron. Sulfate is an essential nutrient required for the crop to utilize Nitrogen in the development of amino acids & proteins. The Sulfate-S form is not acidifying. ALFALFA: 10 T/ac annual yield removes 120 lbs P2O5 and 600 lbs K2O/ac. Multiply ppm NO3-N (Nitrate-N) by 6 to convert to lbs N/ac-ft of soil. Nitrate is available for plant uptake and also can be easily leached from the soil.
