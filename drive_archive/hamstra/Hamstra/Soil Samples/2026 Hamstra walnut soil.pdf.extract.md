Sandy soils have low cation exchange capacity (CEC), have low nutrient retention, low water holding capacity & low pH buffering. Sandy soils can be easily acidified. Excessively well drained sandy soils require spoon fed treatments of dry & liquid fertilizers and pulse irrigation practices to sustain sufficient moisture & nutrients with in the root-zone. All areas are non-calcareous, no free lime is present. Both areas of the South Blk has acceptable Neutral and Acidic pH values. The NE & NW areas have acceptable Acidic pH values. Avoid excessive pH reduction. Top-dressed Lime treatments applied within the irrigated zone can provide pH correction & stabilization with a full season of irrigations and rain water. Acidic pH conditions are required for applied Lime to dissolve and react. The reaction will adjust the pH by buffering to neutralize excessive Hydrogen. Ca will be liberated from the Lime. Maintain soluble salts (EC) above 0.6 dS/m to sustain water penetration. Total salt concentrations above 4.0 dS/m are considered to be excessive. Review irrigation water quality. Review irrigation water quality records to determine chemistry and to assess potential permeability & plant hazards. Low salinity district water may limit water penetration. Maintain soluble Ca greater than TWICE soluble Mg & Na. Supply Ca from high grade sources as needed. Elevated Ca salts can be tolerated at higher salinity thresholds. Maintaining soluble Ca will benefit water penetration, drainage & also enhance nutrient interactions and nutrient availability. Gypsum will supply soluble Ca and Sulfate & provide some short-term pH buffering. Irrigation delivered high grade soluble Ca will benefit infiltration, apply as needed. 100% Gypsum contains 24% Ca & 17% Sulfur as Sulfate, 1 ton will supply 480 lbs Ca & 340 lbs SO4-S. Gypsum has an alkaline pH of 7.8 and can contain 1 to 2% lime and trace Boron. The Sulfate-S form of Sulfur from Gypsum is not acidifying.
