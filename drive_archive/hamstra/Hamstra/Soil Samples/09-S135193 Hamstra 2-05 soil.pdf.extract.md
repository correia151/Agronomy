1 percent OM is equivalent to 40,000 lbs OM per acre-foot of soil. Compost/manure commonly have 50% OM. 40 tons of compost/ac will provide 20 tons of OM, increasing OM by 1.0%. Acceptable slightly Acidic pH value within ideal ranges. Non-calcareous, no free lime is present. Avoid pH reduction. Eventually Lime will be required for pH adjustment. Alfalfa performs better with neutral to alkaline pH conditions. Legumes have a symbiotic relationship with rhizobium bacteria to fix nitrogen. Rhizobium activity decreases in acidic soils. Maintain soluble salts (EC) above 0.6 dS/m to sustain water penetration. Total salt concentrations above 4.0 dS/m are considered to be excessive. Review irrigation water quality records to determine chemistry and to assess potential permeability & plant hazards. Low salinity district water may limit water penetration. Moderate salt concentration (EC) is present. Maintain soluble Calcium (Ca) greater than TWICE soluble Magnesium (Mg) & soluble Sodium (Na). Supply soluble Ca from high grade sources as needed. Elevated soluble Ca salt concentrations can be tolerated at higher salinity thresholds. Pre-plant Gypsum treatments are listed. Maintaining soluble Ca will benefit water penetration, drainage. Supplemental Gypsum treatments can be applied to established stands when needed. 100% Gypsum contains 24% Ca & 17% Sulfur as Sulfate, 1 ton will supply 480 lbs Ca & 340 lbs SO4-S. Gypsum has an alkaline pH and can contain trace amounts of Lime and Boron. The Sulfate-S form is not acidifying, Sulfate is an essential nutrient required for the crop to utilize Nitrogen in amino acids & proteins development. ALFALFA: 10 T/ac annual yield removes 120 lbs P2O5 and 600 lbs K2O/ac. Zn is not considered an economically essential nutrient for Alfalfa. Boron soil treatments of 1 to 3 lbs actual Boron/acre may be considered. Solubor contains 18% Boron. Trace Boron may in irrigation water and in gypsum amendments. Multiply ppm NO3-N (Nitrate-Nitrogen) by 6 to convert to lbs N/ac. High N-P-K fertility values. Estimated Nitrogen Release (ENR) calculates the potential N release from organic matter. Calculate ENR by multiplying TN x 0.08 or 0.16 = ENR lbs/ac-ft. Alternative calculation: %OM x 4,000,000 = lbs OM/ac-12 inch. Lbs OM/ac-12 in x 0.05 = Total N/ac-12 in. Expect 2 to 4% of the calculated Total N to become available during the season.
